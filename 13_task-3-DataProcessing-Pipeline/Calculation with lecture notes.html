6. Core formulas for training estimates

A. Estimate corpus tokens

Corpus tokens = Number of records × Average tokens per record

Example: 310,000 investigation notes × 500 tokens/note = 155,000,000 tokens (155M).

This example assumes 500 tokens per note. Replace it with tokenizer measurements from the actual corpus.

B. Estimate processed training tokens

Processed tokens ≈ Corpus tokens × Epochs

Example: 155M tokens × 3 epochs = 465M token presentations.

This simple estimate assumes every token is processed each epoch. Packing, truncation, filtering, sequence lengths, and data mixtures can change the total.

C. Approximate dense-model training FLOPs

Training FLOPs ≈ 6 × P × D

P = model parameters; D = training tokens processed.

Example: 0.5B parameters × 465M tokens × 6 = 1.395 × 10¹⁸ FLOPs.

A rough rule of thumb for dense transformer training. Architecture, attention implementation, sequence length, optimizer work, checkpointing, and hardware utilization affect real cost.

D. Rough input-inference compute

Input-pass FLOPs/request ≈ 2 × P × Tinput

Example: 0.5B parameters × 300 input tokens × 2 = 3 × 10¹¹ FLOPs/request.

This simplified input-side estimate excludes generated-token compute and important architecture/runtime details. Autoregressive output generation must be benchmarked separately.

E. Estimate training duration

Duration (seconds) ≈ Training FLOPs ÷ Effective FLOPs/second Effective FLOPs/s = Peak FLOPs/s × Utilization

Example: 100 TFLOP/s peak × 30% utilization = 30 TFLOP/s effective. Then 1.395 × 10¹⁸ ÷ 3 × 10¹³ ≈ 46,500 seconds ≈ 12.9 hours.

Illustrative only: 100 TFLOP/s is a hypothetical effective peak for this calculation, not a claim about a particular GPU precision or configuration.

F. Estimate inference GPU count

GPUs = ceil(Peak requests/s × (1 + Headroom) ÷ Benchmark requests/s/GPU)

Example: 2,800 TPS × 1.30 ÷ 500 TPS/GPU = 7.28 → 8 GPUs.

Use measured sustainable throughput at the required P99 latency, context length, output length, batch policy, and availability level. TPS per GPU is model- and hardware-specific.

10. Practical checklist before production sizing

  • Measure actual token counts with the tokenizer for transaction records, investigation notes, SFT examples, and preference pairs.
  • Separate training datasets by objective; deduplicate, redact sensitive data, check labels, and prevent train/test leakage.
  • Confirm whether 12 million records, 310,000 notes, 8,000 reviewed cases, and 5,000 preference pairs are independent, overlapping, or derived subsets.
  • Choose the model and precision first, then benchmark representative training and inference workloads on the target GPU type and region.
  • Measure sustainable requests/s at the required P99 latency, not peak throughput alone; test bursts, retries, failure modes, and autoscaling.
  • Define decision policy and thresholds with fraud operations. Track false positives, false negatives, reviewer workload, drift, and appeal outcomes.
  • Confirm data residency and retention for datasets, logs, checkpoints, caches, backups, observability, and support access.
  • Estimate full cost: GPU instances, storage, data transfer, managed services, monitoring, redundancy, idle capacity, and retraining cadence.
